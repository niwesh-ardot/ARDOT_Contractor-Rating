The Arkansas record

Arkansas narrowed the use and built the process.

The same concern runs through nearly a century of debate. Contractor responsibility matters, and the more a rating affects bidding opportunity, the stronger the evidence and safeguards must be.

  1. 1930

    Define a responsible bidder

    W. W. Zass emphasized experience, organization, equipment and financial capacity as safeguards for public work.

  2. 1986

    Consider performance in capacity

    AHTD proposed using recent project ratings to adjust bidding capacity. Legal review raised concerns about rulemaking and consistent application.

  3. 2020

    Decline performance-based prequalification

    ARDOT estimated at least 31 additional field positions at the proposed scale and raised concerns about subjectivity, claims and competition.

  4. 2021–25

    Build a feedback program with industry

    ARDOT formed a committee, brought in industry representatives, adapted Washington’s approach and completed seven pilot evaluations. The department adopted the report manual in September 2025.

  5. 2026

    Apply the program to major jobs

    ARDOT’s program covers projects with a contract bid amount of $10 million or more, with discretion to include smaller complicated or specialty projects. The stated use is constructive feedback and improvement.

The ARDOT report

100 / 150

100 is standard performance. 150 is the maximum.

What the Resident Engineer rates

Safety, quality, prosecution of work, cooperation, and project management / supervision.

Final reports are due within 45 days of acceptance. The manual provides District Engineer and State Construction Engineer review, contractor response and a two-stage appeal through DocExpress.

The TRB study

A common process. Different consequences.

The study found a recurring pattern across agencies. Tie criteria to the contract, document the work, explain the rating, review it, and give the contractor an opportunity to respond.

Arkansas · Feedback

Five weighted areas, with 100 as standard and 150 as the maximum. The report provides project-specific feedback and identifies areas for improvement.

Washington · Qualification and capacity

Washington uses a similar scoring scale, with 100 as standard and 150 as the maximum. Reports can affect work classes, bidding capacity and qualification status.

Virginia · Qualification status

Quarterly interim evaluations feed annual or final results, which contribute to a rolling five-year Quality Score used for qualification decisions.

South Carolina · Qualification and project eligibility

The model assigns 80 points to calculated measures and 20 to the engineer’s assessment. Scores can affect prequalification and eligibility for designated projects.

Pennsylvania · Bidding capacity

Project ratings are combined into an Ability Factor that affects how much additional work a contractor can bid.

Texas · Contract administration

Interim and final evaluations inform feedback, corrective action and statewide monitoring. The audit shows why completion, evidence and review need active oversight.

Federal CPARS · Future source selection

Contract-specific adjectival ratings, written justification, contractor comments and reviewing-official oversight support future federal source selection.

What the research establishes. The document review identifies design patterns. The seven completed Arkansas pilot evaluations demonstrate that the process was used. The study reports no statistical test of rater agreement or outcome validity for the pilot.

Measurement before consequence

Earn the next use with evidence.

The paper proposes four phases. Each new use depends on the quality of the evidence and the safeguards in place.

01

Current ARDOT program

Feedback

Review project-specific performance with the contractor and document opportunities to improve.

Evidence to advance Reports are complete, timely and traceable to project records.

02

Proposed next step

Validation

Have reviewers independently score the same files and compare ratings with independent project outcomes.

Evidence to advance Reviewers broadly agree and ratings track contractor-attributable outcomes.

03

If validation supports it

Risk management

Try focused oversight or corrective action for documented, recurring risks.

Evidence to advance Oversight is useful, proportionate and fair to firms with limited history.

04

Optional policy decision

Procurement

Consider whether verified history should affect qualification, capacity or project-specific eligibility.

Safeguards to establish Confirm authority, publish the rule, simulate effects on bidder counts and smaller firms, and provide appeal and recovery paths.

What should ARDOT track during validation?
Suggested validation checks from the paper
QuestionEvidence to collect
Are reports usable?Completion and timeliness, traceable records for nonstandard scores, and reviewer returns.
Do reviewers agree?Independent scoring of the same files, criterion-level agreement, and patterns by district, evaluator, work class and firm size.
Can contractors challenge errors?Comments, appeals, overturns and the reasons scores changed.
Do ratings track outcomes?Independent evidence of attributable delay, rework, nonconformance, safety, claims, closeout or postconstruction defects.

Discussion

Would ARDOT benefit from a focused validation review?

Invite Construction, Districts and Program Management to review a sample of completed evaluations together.

Bring leadership a baseline on report completion and evidence quality, a measure of reviewer agreement, and a recommendation on whether to refine the process or test a limited oversight use.

Three questions to decide together

  1. What would make the results credible?

    Which project types, districts and independent outcomes should the sample cover?

  2. Who should own the review?

    Who can lead calibration, independent scoring and evidence checks across districts?

  3. What would justify a next step?

    What findings would support a limited oversight trial, and what findings would send the process back for improvement?
